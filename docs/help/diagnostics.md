# Capacity Diagnostic

Run a three-week Capacity Diagnostic for a client: the people in scope, their tasks and answers, the conversations, the Capacity Map, the first win and the readout, all in one place.

The pages are on no menu. Go to `/diagnostics` for every engagement, or `/diagnostics/clients` for the businesses you run them for. **Diagnostics**, **Across clients** and **Clients** at the top of each switch between them.

## Across clients

**Across clients** adds up how the diagnostics have gone: how many met the guarantee, how many chose the sprint or declined, the average hours back a week, measured and projected, the average hours a week carried between systems by hand, and the share of people who use AI tools they found themselves. The last two show a dash until a published readout has systems or AI answers in it. The same figures follow by industry and by source. Only published readouts count toward them; a diagnostic still running is counted as running.

## Add a client

On **Clients**, select **Add a client** and fill in the business, the owner, its size and where it is. A client here is not a company on AiMS HQ. **Edit** on the client's page changes the details. **Archive** puts a client away without deleting anything, and **Restore** brings it back.

## Start a diagnostic

On the client's page, select **Start a diagnostic**. It opens on the first step. Each step is one card with **Back** and **Next** at the foot. The steps along the top go to any step directly.

1. **Pre-flight.** List the tools the group uses and the IT contact, then tick what is done. **Next** waits for **Access secured**: no access by kickoff means the kickoff moves. Below that is the **systems walkthrough**, a 30-minute conversation with the IT or admin contact. Its four questions are on screen. **Add a system** for each one the people in scope work in every week: its name, kind, what lives there and who looks after it. **Add a link** for each way information moves from one system to another: what moves, and whether it is typed in again, exported and imported, or connected automatically. Put what breaks or gets missed (question 4) in the link's notes. **Edit** opens either one again. You can delete a link any time, and a system once no task or link uses it.
2. **Kickoff.** Enter the kickoff and readout dates, the guarantee, the scope and the Capacity Destination. Add the people in scope as a list, one per line: name, role, email. Then edit the owner's pledge, select **Copy** to paste it into their email, and **Mark as sent** once they have sent it. **Draft in the owner's voice** rewrites the message from the kickoff answers, keeping the promises: no job losses for 12 months, the 45 minutes it asks, and that individual answers stay with you. It replaces what's in the box, so read it before you copy it.
3. **Survey.** Select **Enter survey** beside a person and type in their answers: who they are, up to ten tasks with hours, energy and judgment, and the three open questions. Under each task, **Where does this happen?** picks one of the systems, and **Does this mean copying information from one system to another?** asks for the two systems when the answer is yes. Both are optional. Choose **Add a system** in either list for one the walkthrough missed. Then the AI questions: one row per tool (what it is, whether the company provided it or they found it themselves, what they use it for, how often, and what they would never put into one), or tick **None**. After **Save survey** the drawer moves on to the next person without one. Under it, **Every task** lists everyone's tasks, biggest first. Enter the volume check (units a week, minutes per unit) against the biggest ones, set data risk and build hours, and select **Roll up titles** so the same work done by different people shares one name. You can change any name it gives.
4. **Conversations.** The guide is on the left; read the opening line to each person first. Pick the person and type your notes on the right; each box saves when you leave it. Tick **Had a conversation**, and enter the hours a week this person's time back goes to the Capacity Destination. To be walked through it, select **Run a capacity conversation** above the notes: read the opening line, then take each question in turn. Type what the person said and press **Add to notes**; their words go into the notes under the question, and you get one follow-up to ask, or a sign the answer is enough. **Finish** after question 5 fills their best work and where they would put the hours, where those are empty, and ticks **Had a conversation**. Check both, since they are drafted from the answers. To keep a line for the readout, select the words in the notes and press **Pull a quote**, or press **Suggest quotes** to have lines picked from the notes, word for word. Under **Lines for the readout**, **Approve** the ones the owner may see and choose whether a name goes with each. A line has no name unless you give it one. The readout uses the first three you approve.
5. **Build.** The Capacity Map shows every task by energy and judgment, sized by hours. A filled dot is volume-checked; a hollow one is the person's estimate; a dashed ring means the task copies information between systems. Beside it, the systems map shows each system as a box and each way information moves as an arrow: a heavy arrow is typed in again or exported and imported, labelled with the hours a week the copying tasks spend on it, and a thin one is automatic. Under **Systems and AI**: the connections carried by hand, biggest first; any copying no link covers yet, so you can add that link on Pre-flight; and how many use AI tools, with what they use them for, which is for you only. The shaded corner is the candidates, scored in the table below it, with the top next wins under that. Select **Choose first win** to pick a pattern and the task it serves and enter the baseline. Once it is in use, enter the minutes per unit after and the Friday that ends its first full week: the measured hours back, the recovery rate, the projection and the guarantee fill in. Under **Guardrails snapshot**, enter the data the builds would touch and three rules, one per line. **Retyping removed** is in the pattern library for a first win on a connection carried by hand. Record the midpoint check with the owner at the bottom.
6. **Readout.** **Open the readout** shows it as the owner will read it; **Download the PDF** gives you the file to send. Set the 90-day roadmap (three next wins, the week each lands and the champion to train) and press **Save**. Once a diagnostic has systems or AI answers, the readout's Guardrails section becomes the **Systems and AI Snapshot**: the systems map, the top three connections carried by hand with their hours, how many use AI tools and which tools, the data at risk and the three rules. A readout already published keeps the section it was published with. **Write the narrative** drafts a short paragraph for each section from the figures; edit any of them, and each saves when you leave it. A section left empty after writing came back with a number that isn't in the readout, so write that one yourself. When it reads right, press **Publish the readout**. You need the first win's measured week first. Publishing is final: the readout, the notes and the quotes stop changing, and the PDF shows what you published. Then record the decision: **They chose the sprint** or **They declined**. **Download the roadmap as Markdown** gives you the roadmap, the next wins and the Capacity Destination to set up a sprint by hand.

**Archive** at the foot of a diagnostic puts it away.

## Who sees what

Only system admins on AiMS HQ can open these pages. Individual ratings, answers and notes stay with you. The owner gets the readout PDF and nothing else: combined results, the quotes you approved, and roles in place of names. AI use reaches them as counts and tool names only, never who uses which tool or who uses one of their own.
